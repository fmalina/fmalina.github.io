I build software that survives. For the last twenty years I've been helping organisations rescue ageing systems, untangle complicated data and deliver software that remains fast, maintainable and pleasant to work on long after the original developers have moved on. Most of my work has been in Python, Django and PostgreSQL, ranging from government and healthcare to finance, energy trading and semantic knowledge systems. Along the way I've also founded products of my own, designed taxonomies, optimised search, built developer tooling and spent countless hours improving performance instead of adding unnecessary complexity.

A few things that matter to me

I prefer software that is understandable over software that is fashionable. I enjoy reducing complexity more than adding features, deleting code more than writing it, and leaving systems in a better state than I found them. Outside computing I spend much of my time restoring old buildings, working with timber, exploring mountain landscapes and building practical things by hand. Those interests have influenced how I write software: keep it simple, make it durable and avoid unnecessary moving parts.
